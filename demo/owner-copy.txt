8. ARCS — useful, but it should support the connection (landlord pages 15–18; renter pages 15–20)
The current ARCS screens take up a lot of space and feel more technical than the rest of the app. We need the four strands to be understandable without making people feel scored or judged. Compatibility is our main innovation; ARCS is one way we help people share and understand relevant evidence.
For landlords, please don’t present the four ARCS categories as a rigid ranking that determines who gets seen. We discussed selecting a small number (up to five) of preferred evidence types as preferences, not compulsory eligibility hurdles. Property-specific practical requirements can be separate.
The “Accept / Prefer / Not needed” controls need a short explanation that not having a preferred document does not automatically rule out a renter. 
For renters, shorten the technical paragraphs about unsaved drafts and Phase 3. For example: “Choose the evidence that best reflects your circumstances. You don’t need every type of document.”
Community should be about community ties and support — universities, clubs, professional networks, volunteering, references and support arrangements — not “how you fit the household”. Household preferences are separate.
Please show a populated, readable summary on renter profiles, not four empty ARCS boxes. Private files must remain private; the public profile should show only appropriate evidence types/statuses.
